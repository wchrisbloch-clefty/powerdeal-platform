## Detailed Battle Cards

### Wärtsilä / INNIO Jenbacher (Recip Engines)
**Where they win**: Flexible load following, peaking applications, lower upfront $/kW, strong installed base and service network, financing relationships.

**Where we win**: 
- Any non-attainment zone — they cannot permit without NSR, BACT, and NOx offsets
- Zero planned downtime vs. their scheduled maintenance windows (typically 2–4 weeks/year)
- No combustion → no safety concerns around H2S, hydrocarbon exposure in refinery environments
- Efficiency: our heat rate vs. their 7,500–8,500 BTU/kWh

**Watch out for**: Incumbent relationships with refinery reliability engineers. Wärtsilä has deep Gulf Coast installed base. Don't fight on familiarity — fight on permitting and emissions.

**Key question to plant**: "What does your TCEQ NSR review timeline look like for a recip installation in HGB?"

---

### GE LM Series / Solar Turbines (Aero Turbines)
**Where they win**: Large single-unit capacity (10–50 MW in one unit), CHP/steam generation for process heat, established financing and EPC relationships.

**Where we win**:
- Same permitting constraint as recips — NSR required, 18–24 month timeline in non-attainment
- No planned outages vs. their hot section inspections and major overhauls
- Modular — we can scale incrementally; they require large capital commitment upfront

**Watch out for**: CHP/steam value is real. If the customer has a significant steam load and is replacing aging cogen, the steam angle requires explicit response. Our answer: SOFC + waste heat recovery is the path, or separate steam solution.

---

### Capstone Green Energy (Microturbines)
**Status**: Check current financial health before citing — Capstone has had restructuring challenges. Search before any customer conversation.

**Where they win**: Small footprint, lower capex for small loads (<1 MW), distributed micro-installations.

**Where we win**: 
- Low NOx ≠ zero NOx. In HGB, even "low NOx" microturbines may trigger NSR above certain thresholds.
- Efficiency advantage significant at scale.
- They don't scale to the MW+ loads that drive our ICP.

---

### FuelCell Energy (MCFC)
**Where they win**: Carbon capture applications (molten carbonate is uniquely suited), niche utility-scale projects, some government/DOE relationships.

**Where we win**:
- SOFC efficiency advantage at industrial scale
- Faster deployment and modular scalability
- Stronger commercial fleet and reference base (confirm current via web search)
- MCFC operating temperature and electrolyte management creates different O&M profile

---

### Grid / ERCOT Alternative
**The real competitor in most deals** — the customer's default is to wait for the grid.

**Their argument**: No onsite equipment, no fuel management, no OEM relationship, no capital commitment.

**Our kill shot — three facts**:
1. ERCOT large-load queue: 4–7 years current wait in most zones (search current before citing)
2. Uri: Feb 2021 cost Texas industrial customers an estimated $100B+ in losses. Another weather event is when, not if.
3. ERCOT real-time price spikes: ORDC mechanism creates price volatility that BTM generation eliminates.

**Quantify their grid exposure**: 
- Queue delay cost = $/month of delayed production × months in queue
- Uri scenario cost = what did Feb 2021 cost them (ask directly)
- Rate escalation: ERCOT retail rates have escalated [X]%/year — search current data

## Positioning Rules
1. Never disparage competitors by name unprompted. Lead with what combustion/grid *can't do*.
2. When asked directly about a competitor, acknowledge where they win, then pivot to four-lever advantage.
3. Use competitor limitations as Challenger teaching moments: "Here's something worth understanding about your alternatives..."
4. Always search for current competitor announcements before any customer meeting.
