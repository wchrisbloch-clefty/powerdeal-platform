# Non-Attainment Permitting Analyzer

The permitting advantage is often the deal-maker in HGB and Baton Rouge. Quantify it.

## Step 1 — Establish Geography and Zone Status

**Web search required:**
- Search: "[county/city] ozone non-attainment status 2025 2026"
- Search: "HGB Houston Galveston Brazoria non-attainment 8-hour ozone"
- Search: "Baton Rouge ozone non-attainment status"
- Search: "[state] NOx SIP call industrial permit"

Determine:
- Is the facility in a designated non-attainment area? (HGB, Baton Rouge, other)
- Which standard: 8-hour ozone? PM2.5? Both?
- Current classification: Marginal / Moderate / Serious / Severe / Extreme
- Remaining NOx budget for the facility or county

## Step 2 — Combustion Permitting Analysis

For any combustion alternative (recip engine, turbine, microturbine):

**In non-attainment zones:**
- New Source Review (NSR) required for significant NOx emitters
- Best Available Control Technology (BACT) required
- Offset requirement: must buy or retire NOx credits (if available)
- Timeline: NSR review typically 18–36 months in HGB
- Cost: NOx credit prices in HGB range — search current market rate
- Risk: Permit may be denied if NOx budget is exhausted

**TCEQ pathways for combustion:**
- Standard Permit: Limited applicability, NOx thresholds apply
- Permit by Rule (PBR): Low-emission sources only — combustion rarely qualifies at industrial scale
- Case-by-case NSR: Full review, longest timeline, highest cost
- Title V: Required if above major source threshold — adds compliance burden

**Search for:** "[customer company] TCEQ permit application" to check if they've already tried

## Step 3 — SOFC Permitting Advantage

SOFC is electrochemical conversion — no combustion, no NOx, no SOx.

**Permitting implications:**
- No NSR trigger: SOFC emissions are below de minimis thresholds for NOx/SOx
- No BACT required: Not a combustion source
- No offset requirement: No NOx credits needed
- Permit pathway: Typically standard permit or PBR — months, not years
- Timeline advantage vs. combustion NSR: 18–30+ months faster
- Cost advantage: No NOx credit purchase (current HGB NOx credit price: search current)

**Quantify the advantage:**
```
Combustion permit timeline: 18–36 months (HGB NSR typical)
SOFC permit timeline: 3–9 months (standard permit or PBR)
Timeline advantage: 12–27 months
Revenue at risk during delay: [$/month from four-lever analysis] × timeline advantage
NOx credit cost avoided: [credit price $/ton] × [estimated NOx tons/yr for equivalent combustion capacity]
```

## Step 4 — Challenger Insight for This Account

Write the specific permitting insight to open with:

> "Your [county] facility is in [non-attainment zone] — 8-hour ozone, [classification]. Any combustion-based generation above [threshold] kW requires full NSR review. The last comparable permit in HGB took [X] months and cost [X] in NOx credits — and three projects in the last 24 months were denied outright. We bypass that entirely. Here's what that means for your [expansion timeline / production plan / capital project]."

Customize with:
- Specific non-attainment classification and current status
- Recent combustion permit denials in the same zone (search TCEQ public records)
- Their specific facility location relative to the zone boundary
