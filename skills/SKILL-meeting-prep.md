# MEETING PREP — Master Skill

> **The rule:** Every meeting in a deal has a different person across the table with a different
> primary fear, a different definition of "win," and a different way they'll kill the deal.
> Prep is not generic — it's persona-specific, stage-aware, and account-calibrated.
> A great first meeting with an engineer fails if you run it like a CFO meeting.
> Know who's in the room. Know what they need to say yes. Know what makes them say no.

## STEP 3 — THE UNIVERSAL PREP STRUCTURE

Every meeting brief outputs these 8 blocks. Length and depth scale with meeting type and time available.

### BLOCK 1 — PERSONA READ
*Who is this person, what do they care about, and what makes them say yes or no.*

For each persona in the room:
- **Primary fear:** What career/professional risk are they managing?
- **Primary win:** What does success look like FOR THEM personally?
- **Primary language:** What words/metrics resonate (NPV vs. reliability hours vs. NOx tons vs. contract language)?
- **Skepticism pattern:** How will they push back and why?
- **Mode:** Are they a Champion, Economic Buyer, Technical Buyer (evaluator/screener), Coach, Blocker, or Influencer? (Miller Heiman)
- **Response mode:** Are they in Growth (want to move), Trouble (urgent pain), Even Keel (no urgency), or Overconfident (think they've solved it)? → Each requires a different approach.

### BLOCK 2 — MEETING GOAL
*One sentence. What does "winning this meeting" mean? What gate does it advance?*

Format: "Walk out of this meeting with [specific deliverable/commitment/information] that advances us from [current stage] to [next stage]."

Never: "Build the relationship." That's a byproduct. Name the gate.

### BLOCK 3 — OPENERS
*2–3 options. Challenger-style. Teach before pitching. Pick one based on room read.*

Each opener must:
- Lead with an insight about THEIR business, not our product
- Be grounded in real data (rate filing, grid event, regulatory trigger, peer move, their own earnings)
- Create a "that's interesting" moment, not a "tell me more about your product" moment
- Match the persona's language (engineer = reliability data; CFO = dollar exposure; sustainability = regulatory gap)

### BLOCK 4 — CORE QUESTIONS
*The 6–10 questions this meeting must answer. SPIN-sequenced.*

**Situation (2–3):** Confirm what you think you know. Never ask what's on their website.
**Problem (2–3):** Surface the pain. Specific, not hypothetical.
**Implication (2–3):** Quantify the cost of the problem. Let them do the math.
**Need-Payoff (1–2):** Let them articulate the value of solving it. They sell themselves.

Decision process questions woven in naturally — not interrogation, conversation.

### BLOCK 5 — FILLERS
*5 no-wrong-answer questions for when the room goes quiet or gives short answers.*

These are release valves. Easy to answer. Always get them talking. Follow the thread wherever it goes.

### BLOCK 6 — CLOSERS
*3 options: hard, soft, and multi-thread. Pick based on how the meeting went.*

- **Hard close:** For warm rooms — specific next step with a date.
- **Soft close:** For cautious rooms — "what would make next meeting worth your time?"
- **Multi-thread ask:** Always. "Who else should be in the room for the next conversation?"
- **Urgency anchor:** Always. "Is there a deadline, budget cycle, or milestone we should be aware of?"

### BLOCK 7 — LANDMINES
*The 3–5 things that could blow up the meeting or kill the deal. With defuse moves.*

Sourced from:
- Vertical-specific landmines (defense security gates, refinery HSE veto, hyperscaler clean-energy clause)
- Persona-specific landmines (CFO anchors on $/kWh; engineer asks for reference site; legal asks for insurance cert)
- Account-specific landmines (pulled from Spine: known risks, single-thread exposure, incumbent relationships)
- Situational landmines (deal stage — too early to show price, too late to introduce new personas)

### BLOCK 8 — MUST-WALK-OUT-WITH
*5 specific things. If you get all 5, the meeting was a win regardless of how it felt.*

Always includes:
1. The quantified pain number (their words, their math)
2. One new stakeholder name (break the single thread)
3. The dominant decision driver for this persona
4. One component of the decision process (who, what threshold, what step)
5. A named next step with a date

## STEP 4 — PERSONA PLAYBOOKS (apply to Blocks 1–4)

### PERSONA 1: ENERGY MANAGER / UTILITY MANAGER
**Their world:** Owns the energy spend, the utility relationship, and the rate strategy. Measured on cost per MWh and reliability uptime. Increasingly owns sustainability reporting too.
**Primary fear:** Surprise rate spike or outage that lands on their desk. Getting locked into something their CFO later questions.
**Primary win:** Cost certainty + a story they can tell up the chain with numbers.
**Language:** $/MWh, demand charges, 4CP, capacity tags, rate escalators, PPA, utility tariff.
**Mode:** Usually Growth or Trouble. They called the meeting or took yours — they're curious.
**Methodology angle (Challenger):** Lead with rate trajectory math they haven't modeled. Teach them their own exposure before showing the solution.
**Key questions:**
- "What's your all-in $/MWh today — blended across energy, demand, transmission, ancillary?"
- "How are you modeling rate escalation in your budget? What's your 3-year assumption?"
- "When you think about energy risk here — is the bigger concern cost trajectory or supply reliability?"
- "What does your utility contract look like — standard tariff or negotiated industrial rate?"
- "If power costs rise 15% next year, where does that land on your problem priority list?"
**Landmines:** Don't over-quote $/MWh before you know their baseline — you may be quoting against a subsidized rate. Don't assume they have capital authority — they often need CFO sign-off for anything above $1M.

---

### PERSONA 2: FACILITIES MANAGER / DIRECTOR OF FACILITIES
**Their world:** Owns the physical plant. Manages contractors, maintenance, uptime, safety, and site security. Operational, not strategic. Doesn't want surprises on-site.
**Primary fear:** Something new on their site that breaks, creates liability, or causes a safety incident.
**Primary win:** Something that makes their job easier and their plant run cleaner without adding complexity.
**Language:** Uptime, maintenance windows, footprint, BTU, one-line diagram, interconnection, O&M, NFPA, reliability.
**Mode:** Often Even Keel or skeptical. They've seen vendors come and go. Prove it works before they trust it.
**Methodology angle (Gap Selling):** Current state = maintenance costs + downtime hours + aging equipment. Future state = zero planned downtime, remote monitoring, no combustion. Make them quantify the gap themselves.
**Key questions:**
- "Walk me through how power is distributed on this site — any existing generation assets, backup systems?"
- "What does a typical maintenance window cost you in production downtime?"
- "Who handles O&M on your current power infrastructure, and what does that annual spend look like?"
- "What's your biggest operational headache with the current power setup?"
- "When you think about adding new infrastructure on-site, what's the process — facilities, safety review, security?"
**Landmines:** Don't minimize the installation footprint question — they want to know exactly where it goes. Don't skip the safety/NFPA conversation — combustion-free is your ace here, play it.

---

### PERSONA 3: PRINCIPAL ENGINEER / RELIABILITY ENGINEER
**Their world:** Owns the engineering design, reliability standards, and technical specs. Skeptical of vendor claims. Wants data, not slides. Will validate everything independently.
**Primary fear:** Specifying something that fails in service and traces back to their recommendation.
**Primary win:** A solution that meets or exceeds the spec AND they can defend technically.
**Language:** MTBF, availability, five-nines, N+1, one-line diagram, fault current, IEEE standards, SCADA, heat rate, efficiency curve, degradation rate.
**Mode:** Technical Buyer (screener). They're deciding if we pass the technical gate, not if we win the deal. Pass the gate first.
**Methodology angle (SPIN):** Don't pitch — diagnose. Ask about their reliability spec first. Then show how the product meets it. Let the spec do the selling.
**Key questions:**
- "What's the availability spec for this application — what does your uptime requirement look like?"
- "Do you have an existing reliability standard or a template you apply to onsite generation assets?"
- "What does your one-line look like today — utility feed, substation, distribution to critical loads?"
- "Have you run any feasibility work on onsite generation for this site? What did it say?"
- "What's your biggest technical concern about a solid oxide fuel cell in this environment — let's address it directly."
- "What would a successful technical validation process look like on your end — what do you need to see?"
**Landmines:** Don't dodge the technical question — answer it directly or admit you'll get back to them with data. Don't lead with slides — ask what they want to see first. Don't oversell availability — let the five-nines spec speak.

---

### PERSONA 4: ELECTRICAL ENGINEER
**Their world:** Owns the electrical design, interconnection, protection scheme, and power quality. Detail-oriented. Wants drawings, specs, and IEEE compliance.
**Primary fear:** Integration failure, fault current problems, protection miscoordination, or IEEE 1547 non-compliance.
**Primary win:** A clean integration that passes their protection study with no surprises.
**Language:** kV, MVA, fault current contribution, anti-islanding, IEEE 1547, interconnection study, protection relay, SCADA, paralleling, synchronization.
**Mode:** Technical Buyer. Must pass this gate or the deal dies in engineering.
**Key questions:**
- "Can you walk me through the existing one-line — what's the utility connection point and substation configuration?"
- "What's the available fault current at the point of common coupling?"
- "Has your utility done an interconnection feasibility study for this site? What were the results?"
- "What are your protection scheme requirements — are you running SEL, GE, or another relay platform?"
- "What's your anti-islanding and synchronization requirement for parallel operation?"
- "What does the SCADA integration need to look like — does this connect to your plant DCS or a standalone system?"
**Landmines:** Don't guess on fault current or protection specs — defer to the study. Don't imply the interconnection study is simple — acknowledge it has a process. Don't confuse kW and kWh in front of an electrical engineer.

---

### PERSONA 5: CFO / VP FINANCE / FINANCE DIRECTOR
**Their world:** Owns the capital allocation. Every project competes for the same budget. Their job is to maximize return on invested capital while protecting the balance sheet.
**Primary fear:** Committing capital to something with an unclear payback or hidden liability. Looking wrong to the board.
**Primary win:** A deal that improves the financial profile — EBITDA, cost certainty, balance sheet optionality — AND they can defend the model to the board.
**Language:** IRR, NPV, LCOE, payback period, EBITDA impact, capex vs. opex, balance sheet treatment, off-balance-sheet, tax equity, ITC, credit risk, counterparty risk, covenant.
**Mode:** Economic Buyer. This is the signer. Everything else in the deal is preamble.
**Methodology angle (Challenger + Gap Selling):** Lead with the financial exposure they haven't quantified. Show the cost of the current state (rate escalation, reliability risk) in NPV terms before showing the solution.
**Key questions:**
- "How does your energy spend show up in the P&L — is it allocated to cost of goods or treated as overhead?"
- "When you think about a project like this — is the preference capex ownership or an off-balance-sheet structure like a PPA?"
- "What IRR threshold does a project like this need to clear for capital approval?"
- "At what dollar threshold does this escalate to board or investment committee approval?"
- "What's the biggest financial risk you'd want mitigated before you'd be comfortable with this — rate certainty, performance guarantee, counterparty risk?"
- "Has the company done any third-party energy financing or PPA structures before? How did that get approved?"
**Landmines:** Never show a pro forma without their actual utility rate — your math will be wrong. Don't lead with capex — ask the structure preference first. Don't skip the board threshold question — you need to know if this needs a board vote.

---

### PERSONA 6: CHIEF SUSTAINABILITY OFFICER / ESG DIRECTOR
**Their world:** Owns Scope 1 and Scope 2 targets, ESG reporting, and increasingly, the investor/regulator relationship. Under pressure from SEC climate disclosure rules and board-level ESG mandates.
**Primary fear:** Making a public commitment they can't verify or that fails audit. Getting called out on greenwashing.
**Primary win:** A solution that materially moves their Scope 1/2 number AND survives third-party verification.
**Language:** Scope 1, Scope 2, GHG Protocol, SBTi, CDP, TCFD, REC, I-REC, market-based vs. location-based, additionality, materiality, Verified Carbon Standard, net-zero pathway.
**Mode:** Influencer or Technical Buyer. Rarely the signer, but increasingly the veto for clean energy claims. Must satisfy their criteria or the deal won't get ESG credit internally.
**Methodology angle (Challenger):** Teach them the difference between location-based grid power (no credit) and our solution (direct Scope 1 reduction + REC pathway). Most don't know the REC fuel-pathway rule — teach it.
**Key questions:**
- "What's your current Scope 1 baseline for this facility — and what's the committed reduction target by year?"
- "Are you on a science-based target (SBTi) pathway, or is this a company-level commitment?"
- "When you think about Scope 1 reduction here — are you prioritizing direct elimination or offset/REC approaches?"
- "Have you worked with RECs before — do you have a market-based accounting method in place?"
- "How does your sustainability team validate and verify the energy claims in your ESG report — third-party verifier, CDP submission, SEC disclosure?"
- "Is there a clean energy specification this project has to meet to count toward your targets?"
**Landmines:** NEVER present "Class I REC-eligible" without the fuel-pathway condition (RNG or renewable hydrogen). Don't conflate RECs with Scope 1 reduction — they're different accounting mechanisms. Don't oversell the emissions story without confirming their verification method.

---

### PERSONA 7: VP / SVP / C-SUITE EXECUTIVE (CEO, COO, President)
**Their world:** Strategic decisions, not tactical ones. They move fast. They hate wasted time. They want the thesis in the first 60 seconds and the bottom line before the meeting ends.
**Primary fear:** Making a big bet that doesn't pay off or distracts from the core business. Looking uninformed to the board.
**Primary win:** A strategic move that solves a real problem AND tells a great story to investors, board, or regulators.
**Language:** Strategic optionality, competitive differentiation, energy independence, risk-adjusted return, board narrative, peer benchmarking.
**Mode:** Economic Buyer or Executive Sponsor. Getting them in the room means the deal is real. Don't waste it.
**Methodology angle (Challenger + Voss):** Lead with the business insight. Mirror their strategic language back. Don't run a discovery call — have a strategic conversation. They should be doing 70% of the talking.
**Key questions:**
- "Where does energy fit in your overall risk picture right now — is it on the board's radar or still an operational issue?"
- "When you think about the next 3–5 years of power demand for this business, what's the biggest uncertainty?"
- "How are you thinking about the tradeoff between cost certainty and capital deployment right now?"
- "What would energy independence mean for this business strategically — is that a goal or a nice-to-have?"
- "If this project works exactly as planned, what does it unlock for you — expansion, ESG goals, cost structure, something else?"
**Landmines:** Don't run slides — have a conversation. Don't get technical — stay strategic and let engineers handle specs. Don't forget to ask about the next site — they think enterprise, not beachhead.

---

### PERSONA 8: PROCUREMENT / STRATEGIC SOURCING
**Their world:** Owns the vendor process, evaluation criteria, and commercial terms. Their job is to protect the company from bad deals and ensure competitive tension. May not understand the technology — but they own the gate.
**Primary fear:** Approving a sole-source that gets challenged later, or a contract with hidden risk.
**Primary win:** A fair, competitive process that lands a good deal they can defend internally.
**Language:** RFP, sole-source justification, preferred vendor, evaluation matrix, terms and conditions, liability cap, indemnification, SLA, performance bonds, insurance requirements.
**Mode:** Technical Buyer (process gate). Not the decision-maker — the gate-keeper. Win them by making their job easier, not by selling around them.
**Methodology angle:** Help them write the RFP criteria in a way that our non-combustion, domestic supply chain, and five-nines availability naturally differentiates. Frame sole-source as risk management, not vendor preference.
**Key questions:**
- "Walk me through how a project like this typically moves through your procurement process — RFP, sole-source, preferred vendor?"
- "What evaluation criteria does your team typically use for an energy infrastructure project?"
- "Is there a preferred vendor list we'd need to be on, or is this an open evaluation?"
- "What's the standard insurance and bonding requirement for an onsite infrastructure project at this scale?"
- "What are the typical redline areas your legal team focuses on for a PPA or EaaS agreement?"
- "Who typically provides final commercial approval — is it you, legal, or does it go to the CFO?"
**Landmines:** Don't bypass procurement or try to go around them — they'll kill the deal on principle. Don't treat the RFP criteria as fixed — they're often shaped early. Don't skip the sole-source conversation — make the case for why competitive bidding on this category doesn't work the way it does for commodity procurement.

### PERSONA 9: LEGAL COUNSEL (In-House or Outside)
**Their world:** Owns contract risk, liability allocation, and regulatory compliance. Measured on what goes wrong, not what goes right. Risk-averse by profession.
**Primary fear:** Signing something with unquantified liability, ambiguous performance obligations, or regulatory exposure.
**Primary win:** A clean contract where every risk is allocated, every obligation is defined, and their client is protected.
**Language:** Force majeure, indemnification, limitation of liability, liquidated damages, representations and warranties, material adverse change, cure period, termination for cause, regulatory compliance, governing law.
**Mode:** Technical Buyer (legal gate). They don't buy — they approve or block. Getting their sign-off means the deal closes.
**Methodology angle (Voss):** Label their concerns explicitly. "It sounds like the performance guarantee language is the area that needs the most precision." Mirror. Then: "What would it take to get to language that protects your client while giving us certainty on performance obligations?"
**Key questions:**
- "What's your typical review timeline for an agreement of this scope — from redline to execution?"
- "Which sections do you typically focus on first — performance obligations, liability cap, or termination provisions?"
- "What's the standard liability cap structure your client uses for infrastructure agreements?"
- "How does your team handle force majeure in energy agreements — are there specific carve-outs you require?"
- "Is there existing contract language from a prior energy agreement your team would want to use as a starting point?"
- "What's the approval chain from your markup to final execution — does this go to the board?"
**Landmines:** Never minimize legal risk — acknowledge it, then solve it. Don't rush them — legal on a time crunch makes bad decisions that delay further. Do provide markup-ready templates with pre-negotiated fallback positions to make their job easier.

---

### PERSONA 10: EHS / HSE DIRECTOR (Environmental, Health & Safety)
**Their world:** Owns regulatory compliance, permit obligations, and safety incidents. One violation can mean plant shutdown. Conservative by necessity.
**Primary fear:** A new technology on-site that creates an incident, triggers an inspection, or violates a permit condition.
**Primary win:** A solution that reduces their compliance burden and gives them something positive to report to regulators.
**Language:** TCEQ, LDEQ, SCAQMD, NSR, BACT, NOx budget, permit-by-rule, Title V, hazardous area classification, OSHA PSM, risk management plan, emissions inventory.
**Mode:** Technical Buyer (compliance gate) or blocker. In non-attainment zones, they may be the most important person in the room.
**Methodology angle (Challenger):** Teach them the no-combustion permitting advantage. Most EHS directors in non-attainment zones are fighting a losing battle with NOx budget headroom — show them a path out.
**Key questions:**
- "What's your current air permit situation at this facility — Title V, minor source, or permit-by-rule?"
- "Are you in a non-attainment zone for NOx or particulates — and is that limiting what you can add on-site?"
- "What does a new combustion-based generation source require to permit here — have you run that analysis?"
- "Have you had any NSR or BACT reviews triggered by prior projects? How long did those take?"
- "What does your annual emissions inventory look like for Scope 1 — and is there a commitment to reduce it?"
- "How does your facility handle OSHA PSM or RMP requirements — is there a risk management plan in place that would cover new energy infrastructure?"
**Landmines:** No-combustion is your strongest card — play it early. Don't assume they know the NSR/BACT distinction — teach it. Don't skip HAZLOC classification — in refining and chemical, the fuel cell's location relative to classified areas matters.

---

### PERSONA 11: PROGRAM MANAGER / VP OPERATIONS
**Their world:** Owns production schedules, program milestones, and operational continuity. Power is infrastructure — it supports the mission, not the mission itself. Until it fails.
**Primary fear:** A power event that delays a program milestone, triggers a customer penalty, or makes them explain a production gap.
**Primary win:** Energy infrastructure that is invisible — it just works, and they never have to think about it.
**Language:** Program schedule, production uptime, milestone, OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness), force majeure, critical path, recovery time objective (RTO), MTTR.
**Mode:** User Buyer (daily impact evaluator). They'll live with this system for 15 years. Their input matters for the technical requirements, and they can become a powerful internal champion.
**Key questions:**
- "When you think about power risk for this program — what would a 4-hour outage actually mean for your schedule?"
- "Have you had any power events in the last few years that created production impact? What did that cost?"
- "What's your recovery time objective if this facility loses power — how long before you're back up?"
- "Is there a production expansion tied to this program that changes your power demand?"
- "What does the operations team need to see before they'd be comfortable with new power infrastructure on-site?"
**Landmines:** Don't get too technical — they care about uptime, not fuel cells. Don't ignore the integration timeline question — commissioning during a program execution period will get rejected.

---

### PERSONA 12: SECURITY DIRECTOR / FSO (Facility Security Officer) / CISO
**Their world:** In defense and government contexts, owns physical security, classified facility compliance (DCSA/NISP), personnel security, and cyber. Nothing happens on-site without their approval.
**Primary fear:** A non-cleared vendor, an unsecured data connection, or a physical access event that triggers a DCSA violation or loss of facility clearance.
**Primary win:** A technology partner with a domestic supply chain, clean security posture, and a process that maps to their compliance framework.
**Language:** DCSA, NISP, SCI, SCIF, classified area, cleared contractor, foreign ownership control (FOCI), domestic manufacturing, supply chain security, CMMC, cyber-physical.
**Mode:** Veto authority. They can kill a deal at any stage with one security flag. Surface them early.
**Methodology angle:** Lead with domestic, rare-earth-free, no foreign-ownership exposure. This is the single best value prop for defense security personas — frame it as supply chain security, not just clean energy.
**Key questions:**
- "When does your security team typically get involved in an infrastructure project like this — early feasibility or closer to contracting?"
- "Are there classified area adjacency concerns we should map from the start — where would power infrastructure sit relative to SCI or restricted spaces?"
- "What's your supply chain security requirement for vendors — do you require cleared contractors, domestic manufacturing, or CMMC compliance?"
- "Is there a FOCI concern with any non-domestic energy technology on-site?"
- "What's the cyber-physical security requirement for a networked infrastructure system — SCADA, remote monitoring, data sovereignty?"
**Landmines:** Do not send non-cleared personnel into a cleared facility without pre-coordination. Do not propose cloud-connected monitoring without addressing data sovereignty first. The domestic/no-China-supply-chain story is your strongest asset here — lead with it every time.

---

### PERSONA 13: INVESTMENT COMMITTEE / BOARD / PE SPONSOR
**Their world:** Capital allocation at the portfolio or enterprise level. They see dozens of projects. They want return, risk clarity, and strategic fit.
**Primary fear:** Approving a project that underdelivers, creates liability, or distracts from the core value-creation thesis.
**Primary win:** A deal that improves the investment's risk-adjusted return, EBITDA multiple, or exit story.
**Language:** IRR, MOIC, EBITDA, cap rate, risk-adjusted return, ESG premium, exit multiple, duration risk, counterparty risk, take-or-pay, balance sheet impact.
**Mode:** Economic Buyer. The final capital gate.
**Key questions:**
- "What return threshold does a project like this need to clear for capital committee approval?"
- "How does the investment committee think about energy infrastructure — strategic capex or operational overhead?"
- "Is there a preferred commercial structure at the portfolio level — ownership, PPA, or operating lease?"
- "What's the ESG lens the committee applies — are there clean energy requirements in the investment mandate?"
- "If this project delivers 15-year cost certainty and reliability, how does that impact the asset's valuation at exit?"
**Landmines:** Don't present before the champion has pre-sold the concept — board cold pitches almost never work. Don't skip the exit story — PE sponsors think about what the energy infrastructure does to enterprise value at exit.

## STEP 5 — METHODOLOGY MATRIX (applied invisibly per meeting type)

| Meeting Type | Lead Method | Supporting Methods | The Move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro / First Call | Challenger | SPIN, Gap Selling | Teach → Diagnose → Earn next meeting |
| Lunch & Learn | Challenger | SNAP | Insight-led story → multi-thread stakeholder map |
| Technical Deep-Dive | SPIN | Gap Selling | Diagnose the spec → match to requirement → pass the technical gate |
| Commercial / Economic | Gap Selling | Challenger, SPIN | Quantify the gap with their numbers → build the business case |
| C-Suite / Executive | Challenger | Voss | Business insight → calibrated questions → executive sponsorship |
| CFO / Finance | Gap Selling | Challenger | Financial exposure math → structure options → capital approval path |
| Sustainability / ESG | Challenger | SPIN | Teach the REC pathway + Scope 1 mechanics → align to their target |
| Permitting / EHS | Challenger | Gap Selling | Teach NSR/BACT gap → show the combustion-free path |

## STEP 6 — LANDMINE LIBRARY (situational — apply per persona + vertical)

### Universal Landmines (every meeting)
- **Single-thread exposure:** One name in the room = one point of failure. Always ask for the next name before leaving.
- **Premature pricing:** Quoting $/MWh before you know their baseline is a self-inflicted wound. Diagnose first.
- **Over-pitching to the wrong persona:** Engineers don't want business cases. CFOs don't want one-line diagrams. Read the room.
- **Skipping the decision-process question:** Every meeting should advance the map. If you leave without a new piece of the decision map, the meeting underperformed.

### Defense-Specific Landmines
- Security review is a veto point — surface it at G0, not G3.
- Foreign ownership / FOCI concern with any non-domestic supply chain. Lead with domestic, rare-earth-free story.
- Classified facility access — cleared contractors only. Coordinate before scheduling site visit.
- Program protection — don't propose installation during a critical program execution window.

### Industrial / Chemical Landmines
- HAZLOC classification — fuel cell location relative to classified areas (NEC 500/505) must be addressed early.
- NSR/BACT trigger — any combustion addition in non-attainment triggers review. Frame no-combustion as the escape valve.
- Process continuity — never propose an installation that interrupts production without a phased plan.
- HSE veto — don't skip the EHS Director. In chemical plants, they often have hard veto authority on new site infrastructure.

### Data Center Landmines
- Clean energy clause — hyperscaler sustainability teams often require 24/7 CFE. Know the fuel-pathway rule cold.
- Tier requirements — any downtime during commissioning may violate Tier III/IV SLAs. Plan around it.
- IT/Critical power architecture — the power distribution design must integrate with existing UPS and bypass systems.

### O&G Landmines
- Combustion permit assumption — don't assume they think combustion is the only option. Many haven't been taught differently.
- Flare gas monetization — some upstream accounts want to use associated gas. Gas quality spec must be validated.
- Turnaround window — don't propose major electrical work during a scheduled turnaround or you'll wait 18 months.

### REC / ESG Landmines
- **Never say "Class I REC-eligible" without the fuel-pathway condition.** Pipeline natural gas → APS qualification; RNG or renewable hydrogen → Class I. State this every time.
- Greenwashing risk — ESG directors are hypersensitive to unverifiable claims. Only claim what survives audit.
- Additionality — some sustainability frameworks require new generation (additionality), not existing. Know their framework.
