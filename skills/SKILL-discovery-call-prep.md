# Discovery Call Prep

Output a complete pre-call brief. Never generic — every element must be specific to the named account.

## Step 1 — Account Research (web search required)

Search before writing. Collect:
- Company overview: size, revenue, facilities, recent news
- Power situation: utility provider, known grid constraints, recent expansion announcements
- ESG posture: sustainability reports, stated Scope 1/2 targets, any public emissions commitments
- Recent earnings or press: cost pressure signals, capex plans, reliability incidents
- Geography: HGB/non-attainment zone? ERCOT territory? Gulf Coast / LA corridor?
- Stakeholder intel: LinkedIn the likely attendees if names are known

Flag all data with source and date. Flag anything stale or unverified.

## Step 2 — ICP Pre-Score

Score the account on the five ICP dimensions (1–5 each, threshold 15/25):

| Dimension | Score | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Power need (MW, baseload?) | /5 | |
| Grid pain (queue, constraints) | /5 | |
| Permitting pressure (non-attainment?) | /5 | |
| ESG/regulatory driver | /5 | |
| Financial capacity | /5 | |
| **Total** | **/25** | |

If score < 15: flag as borderline and note what would need to be true to qualify.

## Step 3 — Challenger Commercial Hook

Write the single opening insight for this account — something they don't already know about their own situation. Format:

> "Your [facility/geography] faces [specific constraint — NOx budget, queue position, reliability gap]. Here's what that means for [their specific business outcome — expansion timeline, crack spread, PUE cost, margin]."

This is the first thing said after introductions. Not a product pitch — a business insight.

## Step 4 — Discovery Question Sequence

Structure as SPIN. 8–10 questions max. Ordered to build from situation → implication → need.

**Situation (2–3 questions — confirm what you think you know):**
- Power consumption and load profile questions
- Current utility/grid arrangement
- Existing generation assets (cogen, diesel backup)

**Problem (2–3 questions — surface the pain):**
- Reliability history, outage events
- Expansion or permitting constraints
- Grid queue or substation limitations

**Implication (2–3 questions — quantify the gap):**
- Cost of last outage / what does a week without power cost?
- Revenue or margin impact of power constraints
- What does current permitting timeline mean for their expansion plan?

**Need-Payoff (1–2 questions — let them sell themselves):**
- "If you could get reliable baseload power in 18 months instead of 4 years, what does that unlock?"
- "What would zero planned downtime mean for your maintenance scheduling?"
