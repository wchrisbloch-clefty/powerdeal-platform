# Contract Term Negotiator

Position, fallback, trade map, and exact language. Never generic.

## Step 1 — Identify the Negotiation Context

Establish:
- **Commercial structure**: PPA / Capital sale / EaaS / Tolling
- **Term in dispute**: [$/MWh rate / escalator / term length / performance guarantee / curtailment / other]
- **Their position**: [What they've said or proposed]
- **Our position**: [Current stance]
- **What we know about their underlying concern**: [Career risk / cost certainty / flexibility / other]
- **Deal stage**: [Heads of terms / term sheet / final contract]
- **Their BATNA**: [Grid / combustion / delay / other supplier]

## Step 2 — Apply Negotiation Framework

**Pre-negotiation check:**
1. Is this a value trade or a value destroy? (Trades are fine. Concessions without reciprocity are not.)
2. What's our BATNA if this deal doesn't close? (Affects how hard we hold.)
3. What does this person need to say yes AND look good internally?

**For each disputed term, define:**

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Our anchor position | [Opening stance] |
| Their likely push | [Expected counter] |
| Our fallback | [Acceptable resolution] |
| What we'll trade it for | [Specific concession we'd accept in exchange] |
| What we won't move on | [Hard limit and why] |

## Step 3 — Term-Specific Playbooks

### $/MWh Rate Dispute
- Label: "It sounds like the rate is the number you need to defend internally."
- Mirror their specific language.
- Calibrated question: "How do we make the total economics work for both sides?"
- Redirect: Total cost of ownership including avoided demand charges, 4CP, reliability value, ITC.
- Trade: Lower starting rate for steeper escalator or longer term.

### Term Length (they want shorter)
- "What would need to be true about the first 5 years for you to feel confident extending?"
- Build in contractual extension options at favorable pre-set rates.
- Trade: Shorter initial term at higher rate; include automatic extension with customer opt-out.

### Performance Guarantee (they want higher)
- Never negotiate performance in a vacuum.
- Trade: Higher availability guarantee in exchange for higher rate, longer term, or reduced curtailment rights.
- Structure: Tiered — 95% base, bonus pool above 97%, LD below 92%.

### Curtailment Rights (they want unlimited)
- Minimum take-or-pay volume: "80% capacity factor is the floor for the economics to work."
- Trade: Allow curtailment above 20% in exchange for termination fee or rate adjustment.

### Termination for Convenience (they want it)
- Offer termination for convenience with a breakage fee schedule that recovers our unrecovered capital.
- Structure: Declining breakage fee over term (front-loaded recovery, tapering after year 10).

### Fuel Cost Pass-Through (they want fixed all-in)
- "We can offer a fixed all-in price, but the rate needs to reflect our hedge cost — here's what that looks like."
- Preferred: Index-linked with heat rate guarantee. Fixed rate alternative: add 10–15% risk premium.

### Legal Redlines on Performance
- Escalate to principal-level: "Let's get our teams aligned on the structure before we negotiate the floor."
- Pre-provide markup-ready templates with fallback positions already built in.

## Step 4 — Voss Language Templates

**To reset an anchor:**
> "It sounds like [their stated position] is the number that matters most to you right now. Help me understand — what's driving that specific number?"

**To create a collaborative reframe:**
> "That's right — we should make this work for both sides. What would it take to get there?"

**To hold a position without closing a door:**
> "I hear you. Here's the challenge on our side: [explain constraint without apologizing]. What if we looked at [alternative structure]?"

**To advance past a stall:**
> "What would need to be true about [disputed term] for this to move forward?"
